Trend Slope and Strength: how the series are built

Portfolio return series and sort diagnostics from “Trend Slope and Strength” (Chad Schmerling, Chicago Booth, and Kingsley Yeon, University of Chicago). The paper decomposes a stock’s price chart into two numbers from one regression of cumulative return on time: the R2 is trend strength and the coefficient is trend slope. Sorting on strength first and slope within produces the TS factor, long smooth uptrends and short noisy downtrends. Every series here is generated from the paper-baseline result files by the same code that produces the paper’s tables; nothing is edited by hand.

Protocol

One protocol sits behind every series on this page.

ParameterValue
UniverseCRSP common stock (share codes 10, 11) on NYSE, AMEX, and NASDAQ, price at or above $5

Factor modelFama–French five factors plus Carhart momentum (FF6), Newey–West HAC with 21 lags on daily data

Conventions

  • Units. All returns are simple returns in decimal units (0.01 = 1%), gross of transaction costs, inclusive of dividends and delisting returns.
  • Frequency. Daily files have one row per trading day. Monthly files compound the daily returns within each calendar month and are stamped with the calendar month-end date.
  • Alphas. Long-only portfolio alphas are intercepts on returns in excess of the one-month T-bill. Long-short spreads are self-financing, so spread alphas are intercepts on the raw spread. The excess_of_rf_in_regression column in summary_stats.csv records which convention applies to each row.
  • Annualization. Annualized return is the daily mean times 252; volatility is the daily standard deviation times √252; alphas are annualized percentages.
  • Spread identity. On daily returns, RQ5_SQ5 − RQ1_SQ1 in a portfolio file reproduces the matching TS series to numerical precision. The identity does not survive monthly compounding of each leg separately, which is why the explorer’s cumulative chart compounds the spread itself.
  • Benchmark formation. The explorer’s signal spreads use the paper’s chart window, [t−252, t], matching its horse-race table. The download set also carries a skip-21 variant, [t−252, t−21], the momentum literature’s skip-month convention, in which short-term reversal (prior-month return) is also defined.
  • Index benchmarks. index_benchmarks_{daily,monthly}.csv hold long-only S&P 500 and NASDAQ returns in equal- and value-weighted form, built from CRSP with the same return conventions. S&P 500 membership comes from CRSP’s constituent list (dsp500list); the NASDAQ universe is NASDAQ-listed common stocks (share codes 10, 11) with prior-day price at or above $5, the paper’s price filter, and starts January 1973 when CRSP’s NASDAQ coverage begins. VW weights by prior-day market capitalization daily; EW sets equal weights on the first trading day of each month over the stocks that qualify that day and holds them within the month, which avoids the bid-ask-bounce bias of daily-rebalanced equal weighting. Equal-weighted NASDAQ is dominated by small stocks and is far above the value-weighted index; treat it as a diagnostic, not an investable benchmark.

Files

Headline series
ts_factor_{daily,monthly}.csv carry the TS factor in four specifications (ts_full_ew, ts_full_vw, ts_top500_ew, ts_top500_vw). portfolios25_{full,top500}_{ew,vw}_{daily,monthly}.csv carry all 25 conditional double-sort cells, RQ1_SQ1 through RQ5_SQ5. rolling_ff6_alpha_60m.csv is the trailing 60-month daily FF6 regression at each month-end for the four TS series (annualized alpha, t-statistic, momentum and market betas); the first estimate is January 1968. summary_stats.csv gives full-sample statistics for every published series, including each of the 100 cells (RQ{i}_SQ{j}_{universe}_{weighting}).
Benchmark spreads
benchmark_spreads_{ew,vw}_{chartwindow,skip21}_{daily,monthly}.csv: decile-10 minus decile-1 spreads on the full universe for the R2 family and standard momentum-family signals, each built with the same sorting machinery. Columns: R2 (unsigned R2), SignedR2, TSlope (t-statistic of the slope, a monotone transform of signed R2, so its deciles coincide with SignedR2), Slope, Mom12m, Mom6m, IntMom (months 7–12), STreversal, LRreversal (months 13–60), MRreversal (months 13–36), High52, MomVol, IndMom, ResidualMom, and TrendFactor (Han, Zhou and Zhu 2016). These are the paper’s own implementations; see its horse-race section before treating them as canonical versions of the published factors.
Index benchmarks
index_benchmarks_{daily,monthly}.csv: sp500_ew, sp500_vw, nasdaq_ew, nasdaq_vw, long-only index returns built as described under Conventions. NASDAQ columns are empty before January 1973.
Factor-level strategies
factor_level_ts_{daily,monthly}.csv apply the TS logic to a 17-factor universe (FF6 plus eleven horse-race spreads) in the spirit of Ehsani and Linnainmaa (2022): at each month-end each factor’s own cumulative return is regressed on time. factor_TS is long factors with positive slope and above-median R2, short factors with negative slope and above-median R2; factor_PRET is the plain factor-momentum baseline; factor_R2 sorts on R2 alone. Starts March 1969.
Sort diagnostics
For replication without CRSP access. breakpoints_monthly.csv has one row per formation date and universe: the number of stocks with a valid signal, the lower edges of R2 quintiles 2 to 5, and the lower edges of slope quintiles 2 to 5 inside each R2 quintile (slope in cumulative-return units per trading day; 0.001 is roughly 25% per year). cell_stats_monthly.csv has one row per formation date, universe, and cell: stock count, mean R2, mean and median slope, and mean and median market cap in millions of dollars.

What is deliberately not here

No stock-level data. Permno-level R2, slope, and quintile assignments are derived directly from licensed CRSP daily returns and are not redistributed. The breakpoints and cell statistics above are the aggregate substitutes: with your own CRSP access they let you reproduce the sort exactly. The TAQ retail-flow panel used in the paper’s mechanism section is likewise licensed and not included.
