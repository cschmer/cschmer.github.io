Research

A Century of Meme Stocks and the Price of Coordination

Working Paper

“Meme stocks” are known by their social-media chatter, impossible to identify before the internet age. I instead learn “memeness” from a meme-stock index fund’s holdings using market data alone, so stocks can be scored back to 1926. Memeness identifies coordinated retail mispricing: prices displaced by crowds that congregate faster and more cheaply as coordination technology improves. The displacement corrects at a weekly horizon: a value-weighted five-factor-plus-momentum alpha near 10%/year (t ≈ 7), earned in two weeks and reversed within a month. It persists because arbitraging it means bearing crash risk that realizes on the crowd’s timetable. Both the mispricing and its crash content emerge in force after 2000, exactly when coordination costs collapse; sampled weekly, the correction alpha holds out of sample in 2023–2025.

Trading the Chart: Trend Slope, Trend Strength, and Retail Extrapolation

Working Paper

Retail investors meet a stock through its price chart. I decompose the chart into trend slope and trend strength—the R2 of cumulative returns regressed on time—and sort on strength first, direction within. The resulting TS factor, long smooth uptrends and short noisy downtrends, earns 7.9% annualized six-factor alpha (t = 3.44) in value-weighted CRSP, 1962–2025, and survives spanning by momentum and five related signals. A model of short-sale-constrained chart traders delivers the factor’s asymmetric structure and five confirmed predictions. Retail order flow concentrates in smooth uptrends at formation and rises with trend strength; institutions tilt away, and analyst price targets fall with trend strength—the belief channel is retail-specific. Smooth uptrends barely react to earnings news confirming their trend and then reverse, and the premium concentrates in the first six months after formation, with long-horizon estimates pointing to reversal—the horizon structure extrapolation requires.

Data

Public datasets from my research, browsable online and downloadable as CSV.

Retro Meme-Stock Lists, 1926–2025

Companion data · “A Century of Meme Stocks and the Price of Coordination”

Monthly and weekly top-25 “meme stock” rankings back to 1926, from a classifier trained on the post-2020 meme era using market data alone (returns, options, short interest, retail flow — no social media, no text) and applied backward through history. Six model families, each restricted to the data available from its break year; browse any period, or look up a stock’s every appearance.

Trend Slope and Strength: Portfolio Returns, 1963–2025

Companion data · “Trend Slope and Strength” (with Kingsley Yeon)

Daily and monthly returns to the TS factor — long smooth uptrends, short noisy downtrends — in four specifications, all 25 cells of the conditional R2-then-slope double sort, rolling 60-month FF6 alphas, and decile spreads for fourteen benchmark signals built with the same machinery. Sort breakpoints and per-cell statistics are included so the sort can be reproduced with your own CRSP access; no stock-level data are redistributed.
